# Deviations from the Original Paper

Tracks every difference between this project and Rafailov et al. (2023), "Direct Preference Optimization" (arXiv 2305.18290), plus our choices for anything the paper leaves unspecified. Paper reference values live in [`CLAUDE.md`](CLAUDE.md) §4; our config values live in [`configs/base.yaml`](configs/base.yaml).

**Rule:** add or update an entry in the same change that introduces the difference. Do not delete entries. If a deviation is reverted, set its status to `Reverted` and say why.

**Status values:**
- `Planned`: decided but not yet implemented
- `Active`: implemented
- `Provisional`: implemented, but the value must be revisited on the full-scale GPU runs
- `TBD`: undecided
- `Reverted`: undone; the entry says why

## Deviations

| ID | Area | Paper | Ours | Reason | Expected impact | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Scope | Five experiments: IMDb sentiment, TL;DR summarization, Anthropic-HH dialogue, CNN/DailyMail OOD, human study | **IMDb controlled sentiment only** (Sec 6.1, Fig 2 left) | Focus; this is the cheapest experiment and the only one with ground-truth reward | Replicates only the claim that "DPO optimizes the RLHF objective better than PPO". No claims about summarization or dialogue quality | Active |
| D2 | Baselines | DPO, PPO, PPO-GT (our impl.), PPO-GT (TRL), Unlikelihood, Preferred-FT (22 runs total) | **DPO, PPO, PPO-GT (our impl.)**: 4 + 4 + 4 = 12 runs. Unlikelihood, Preferred-FT and PPO-GT (TRL) are dropped | User wants a DPO-vs-PPO comparison. PPO-GT (TRL) can't exist without TRL (D5) | Fig 2 left has fewer series. Losing PPO-GT (TRL), the paper's weakest PPO-GT variant, doesn't affect the two core claims | Active |
| D3 | Addition | No analysis of training time vs performance (only "every 100 steps until convergence") | **Added:** logging of steps, examples, tokens and wall-clock at every eval, plus statistics relating extra training time to reward and KL (CLAUDE.md §6, `src/dpo_rep/analysis.py`, `scripts/07_analyze.py`) | User-requested extension | Should be none: logging is observational. Timers synchronize the GPU at section boundaries, which adds negligible overhead | Active |
| D4 | Compute / hardware | Not reported | **One NVIDIA A100 on RunPod** (40 or 80 GB; record which in this row once the pod is chosen), bf16 autocast with fp32 weights. `gpu_name` is recorded in every metrics row. Development and smoke tests run on an Apple M2 (MPS, fp32). Setup / launch: `scripts/runpod.sh` | Local machine too slow for gpt2-large PPO (~3 days per PPO run). Brown's Oscar was considered, but undergrad GPUs are older (possibly no bf16, less memory, job time limits) | Absolute wall-clock numbers are specific to the A100, so time-based comparisons are only valid within one GPU type. Estimated full sweep: ~20–28 GPU-hours (not yet measured) | Active (40 vs 80 GB TBD) |
| D5 | Implementation | Authors' own code; PPO-GT (TRL) uses the TRL library | **Written from scratch:** PyTorch only, with HF `transformers` / `datasets` used solely to load weights, tokenizers and IMDb. Own sampler (KV cache), DPO, Bradley–Terry RM, PPO (GAE, clipped losses, value head, adaptive KL), KL evaluation. No TRL, and the authors' reference repo was **not consulted** | User decision: replicate the paper's code from scratch | Unspecified implementation details (the U-rows) are our own choices and may differ from the authors' | Active |
| D6 | Precision | Not reported | bf16 autocast on CUDA, **fp32 master weights** and optimizer states | With lr = 1e-6, updates applied directly to bf16 weights would mostly round to zero | Should be none; this is a standard choice | Active |
| D7 | Dropout | Not reported | **Dropout disabled** in the DPO policy and reference, and in the PPO policy, reference and value head. Left at GPT-2 defaults (0.1) for SFT and RM training | With dropout, log π_θ / π_ref ≠ 1 at initialization and PPO importance ratios are noisy | Should be none | Active |
| D8 | PPO optimizer / lr | Not reported for PPO (App B's RMSprop / lr 1e-6 is stated for DPO); the paper only says its PPO used "further tuned hyperparameters" | AdamW, weight decay 0, value-head lr 1e-4 (separate parameter group), grad clipping 1.0. **Policy lr selected by a tuning sweep (U14)** and applied to all 8 PPO / PPO-GT runs | At smoke scale a fixed lr of 1e-6 barely moved the policy (KL ≈ 0.01). An undertuned PPO would make "DPO dominates PPO" true for the wrong reason, so PPO gets a fair tuning budget | PPO receives lr tuning that DPO doesn't (DPO uses the paper's fixed hyperparameters), which tilts the comparison slightly *toward* PPO and makes a DPO win more meaningful | Active (record the selected lr from `runs/ppo_lr_choice.json` here after the GPU run) |

## Unspecified in paper: our choices

These are not deviations from stated values; the paper simply doesn't specify them. Record each choice here so results can be interpreted correctly.

| ID | Item | Our choice | Rationale | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| U1 | SFT: size of IMDb train "subset", max seq length, lr / batch / optimizer | **20,000** random IMDb-train reviews (seed 0), each followed by EOS and truncated to **128 tokens**. 1 epoch [paper], AdamW lr 1e-5, batch 32 (micro 8), 50-step warmup, no weight decay. Reviews cleaned of `<br />` tags | 128 tokens comfortably covers the 8-token prefix + 48-token completion used later. 20k is most of the 25k train split | Active |
| U2 | Prefix sampling | Take the **first L tokens** of a review, with L ~ Uniform{2, …, 8} per prefix. Preference data: 25k **distinct** IMDb-train reviews (all of them). PPO prompts: fresh random IMDb-train prefixes every PPO step. Eval: IMDb test. Overlap with SFT text is allowed (same split) | "Prefix of a movie review" read as the beginning of the review | Active |
| U3 | Preference-data generation sampling | Temperature 1.0, no top-k / top-p, **max 48 new tokens**. A completion ends at EOS (included) or at the length limit | Pure sampling from π_SFT keeps the pairs on-policy for π_ref. 48 tokens is long enough for a clear sentiment | Active |
| U4 | Tie handling when two completions get equal classifier score | **Exact ties dropped**; the count is logged in `prefs_meta.json` (0 in the smoke run) | Ties carry no preference signal | Active |
| U5 | Reward model: train/val split, optimizer / lr / batch | 5% of **prefixes** held out for validation (all 6 pairs of a prefix stay on one side). AdamW lr 1e-5, batch 64 (micro 16), 100-step warmup. Validate every 500 steps and at each epoch end, keeping the best checkpoint. Scalar head **zero-initialized**, on the last non-pad token. Init from the base `gpt2-large` [paper]. Normalization: subtract the mean RM score over 4,096 SFT samples (E[r] = 0, paper Sec 3) | Splitting by prefix prevents leakage. Zero init gives a loss of exactly ln 2 at start | Active |
| U6 | PPO hyperparameters beyond target KL (lr: see U14) | **Rollout:** 1024 samples per PPO step [paper]. **Optimization:** 4 PPO epochs, minibatch 64, clip 0.2, value clip 0.2, vf_coef 0.1, γ = 1, λ = 0.95. **KL penalty:** per-token −kl_coef·(log π − log π_ref) with the score added on the last completion token; adaptive KL controller (Ziegler et al.) with init 0.2 and horizon 10,000. **Value head:** shared trunk. **Normalization:** scores whitened per rollout batch (our reading of "normalized rewards"); advantages whitened. **Budget:** 150 PPO steps (~154k rollouts) | Ziegler / Stiennon-style defaults | Active (untuned except lr) |
| U7 | DPO: number of epochs / total steps, max lengths, precision | **2 epochs** over all pairs (~4,680 steps at batch 64), micro-batch 16. Lengths are set by U3 (≤ 8 prompt + ≤ 48 completion tokens). No gradient clipping. Precision per D6. The reference model's log-probs are computed online each step | Fixed budget well past expected convergence, so the training-time analysis sees the plateau | Active |
| U8 | Eval: number of test prompts, source split, generation settings | **512** fixed prefixes from IMDb test (seed 1234, cached in `data/eval_prompts_test.json`). Same sampling settings as U3. The **same sampling seed is reused at every eval** (common random numbers). Eval at step 0, then every 100 DPO steps [paper] / every 5 PPO steps | 512 prompts gives a reward SE of ≈0.01. Common random numbers reduce checkpoint-to-checkpoint noise | Active |
| U9 | KL estimator | **Exact** per-token KL over the full vocabulary, summed over completion tokens, at contexts sampled from π_θ (footnote 3, read literally). The single-sample log-ratio estimate is logged too (`kl_sample`) | Lower variance; matches "sum of the per-timestep KL-divergences" | Active |
| U10 | "Until convergence" stopping rule | **No early stopping.** Every run uses a fixed budget (U6, U7). Convergence / plateau is determined after the fact (U12) | Avoids a stopping rule biasing the frontier; the extension needs the post-plateau region | Active |
| U11 | Number of seeds per configuration | **1** (seed 0), as the paper appears to use | Compute | Active |
| U12 | Extension: plateau threshold and curve fit | Plateau = first checkpoint after which the 3-point moving-average reward never improves by more than **ε = 0.01** (≈ 1 SE at 512 prompts). Fit r(t) = a − b·e^(−t/τ) by least squares (scipy `curve_fit`), with 95% CIs from the covariance. Over-optimization flag: over the last 30% of checkpoints, KL rises significantly (p < 0.05) while reward does not. Compute-matched budgets: 12 geometric points | — | Active |
| U13 | PPO eval spacing | Eval every **5 PPO steps** (5,120 rollouts). DPO evals every 100 steps (6,400 pairs) | "Every 100 training steps" is ambiguous for PPO, where one step is a 1024-sample rollout plus 64 optimizer updates; 5 PPO steps processes a similar number of examples | Active |
| U14 | PPO lr tuning protocol | Before the main PPO sweep: **PPO-GT** at target KL 6 (middle of the paper's grid) with lr ∈ **{1e-6, 3e-6, 1e-5}**, **50 PPO steps** each (main runs use 150). Evaluated on **512 validation prefixes from IMDb's unlabeled `unsupervised` split** (disjoint from train and test), so the reported test-set numbers aren't used for selection. Winner: highest eval reward among checkpoints with **KL ≤ 6**; ties go to the lower lr. If the winner is at the edge of the grid, `05b` prints a warning; extend `ppo_tune.lrs` in that direction, re-run the tuning entries, and log the final grid here. The winning lr is used for both PPO (learned RM) and PPO-GT, all target KLs. Tuning runs are named `tune_*`, excluded from reported results, and their table is in `runs/ppo_lr_choice.json` (`scripts/05b_select_ppo_lr.py`) | Gives PPO a fair, documented tuning budget without leaking the test set. Tuning on PPO-GT, not PPO-RM, picks an lr from the optimizer's behaviour rather than from the RM's errors. The 50-step budget may slightly favour a higher lr | Active |

## Notes

- **No LLM judge is needed.** Experiment 1 is scored with the ground-truth sentiment classifier, so the retirement of `gpt-4-0314` (the judge used for TL;DR and HH) doesn't affect this project.
- **IMDb source:** HF dataset `stanfordnlp/imdb` (25k train / 25k test).
